.. _sgi-ip35:

====================================================
SGI IP35: Origin 300, Fuel and the Origin 350 family
====================================================

IP35 is the software platform of SGI's second Scalable Node generation,
built around the Bedrock node controller. QEMU models six products on it:
``origin300`` (board IP45), ``fuel`` (board IP34), and four products on
the IP53 board, ``origin350``, ``onyx350``, ``tezro`` and ``onyx4-host``.
The machine classes are in ``hw/mips/sgi/sn1.c``. Each node is an
``sgi-sn1-node`` (``hw/mips/sgi/sn1-node.c``), and the shared Scalable
Node builder in ``hw/mips/sgi/sn-machine.c`` assembles the nodes from the
compiled catalogue (:doc:`sn`). Every machine boots a stock IP35 PROM that
the user supplies. This page is the hardware contract the IP35 model
layers in ``hw/mips/sgi/models/`` cite: what each product fits, how it is
wired, the physical address map, and where the emulator chose a value
the hardware does not settle. Chip register behaviour is on the part
pages listed under :ref:`sgi-ip35-devices`.

Facts are rated as described in :ref:`sgi-ratings`.

The Origin 300 and Fuel are modelled from manuals and published
captures of those products. The four IP53 products are serial software
profiles. Each fixes a processor, memory, PIC and IO9 population drawn
from one published capture or manual, and fills the gaps with stated
choices. They run the stock PROM and IRIX on the model. They do not
establish that any physical IP53 board is wired the way they are.

Machines
========

Each machine builds one or two Bedrock nodes. ``-M <machine>,topology=``
selects a wiring the catalogue lists. An unknown name, such as
``topology=help``, makes the machine print the topologies it accepts and
exit.

``origin300`` (*established* for the product): an Origin 300 holds two or
four processors and 512 MB to 4 GB (:src:`007-4365-002 p. 1, 10-12`). The
scan guide lists two- and four-processor 500 MHz R14000 configurations
with 2 MB of cache (:src:`108-0263-004 p. 2-18`). A published
two-processor board reports ``IP45_2CPU``, part ``030-1780-001``
revision C, with processors A and C fitted (:src:`nekonomicon-16726611`),
*observed*. The four-processor population fits the remaining positions B
and D on the same two processor interfaces, *inferred*. The 512 MiB
population is the guide's minimum and the only size the model accepts.

``fuel`` (*established* for the product): one processor module, a
500 MHz R14000A option, and two 256 MiB DIMMs as the minimum pair
(:src:`007-4480-001 p. 30-32, 60`). A published IRIX inventory reports
board ``030-1707-003`` revision G and an R14000 revision 2.3
(:src:`tingo-fuel-irix-console`), *observed*. That machine had 1536 MiB.
Pairing its board with the guide's 512 MiB minimum is a model choice. A
second processor is not a Fuel option.

The IP53 products are described in :ref:`sgi-ip35-ip53`.

.. _sgi-ip35-processors:

Processors
----------

The Origin 300 and Fuel fit QEMU's R14000 at 500 MHz with 2 MiB of
secondary cache. The IP53 profiles fit the R16000 with each profile's
clock, cache size and reset mode word (:ref:`sgi-ip53-serial-profiles`).
:doc:`hardware/r10000` describes the identity words, the reset mode word
and how the R16000 inputs are checked.

A Bedrock has two processor interfaces with two processors each. The
machine populates them diagonally: a two-processor node fits interface 0
position A and interface 1 position A first (``sn1-topology.c``), as the
published two-processor board does.

---------------------

``bedrock-revision`` and ``ioc3-subsystem-id``.

   :header-rows: 1

   * - Origin 300
     - ``0x8001``
     - ``0x34`` (``4``)
     - 1.18.5
     - 1
   * - Fuel
     - ``0x4004``
     - ``0x41`` (``A``)
     - none
     - 2
   * - Origin 350, Onyx 350, Onyx 4 host
     - ``0x2001``
     - ``0x37`` (``7``)
     - 1.18.5
     - 0
   * - Tezro tower
     - ``0x2001``
     - ``0x39`` (``9``)
     - 1.48.1
     - 0
   * - Tezro rack
     - ``0x2001``
     - ``0x51`` (``Q``)
     - 1.48.1
     - 0

- Board word bits 15:12: OpenBSD reads them as the product code and
  assigns C-brick 0, Origin 350 2, Fuel 4 and Origin 300 8 (``ip27.h``
  lines 43-46, ``ip27_machdep.c`` lines 165-185), a *software
  expectation*. The low bits are *unknown* on every product. The stock
  PROM uses the low nibble to pick a row of its processor-configuration
  table: 1 on the Origin 300 and 4 on the Fuel select the 500 MHz, 2 MiB
  R14000 rows (:src:`ip35-prom-6.210`), a *software expectation*. On the
  IP53 products the low bits ``0x001`` are a choice. The register is on
  :doc:`hardware/bedrock`.
- L1 type: Kaloz's series maps ``4`` to Origin 300, ``A`` to Fuel, and
  ``9`` and ``Q`` to the Tezro deskside and rack (:src:`lore-kaloz-ip35-35`),
  a *software expectation*. ``0x37`` on the IP53 servers is a choice. No
  captured L1 type reply exists for any of them, *unknown*.
- L1 revision: the Fuel captures report 1.48.1 and 1.14.4, and neither
  is tied to the modelled board, so the Fuel answers the unknown-version
  error unless a launch supplies ``l1-revision=``. The Tezro's 1.48.1 is
  the image in the IP35 patch :src:`ip35-patch-sg0007149`. The others are
  choices.
- Flash machine type: the value written into the PROM's configuration
  block. The PROM's Origin 300 rows carry type 1 and its Fuel rows type 2,
  a *software expectation*. A real Fuel's ``flash -f`` session answers 2
  (:src:`graphica-o350-chimera-notes`), *observed*. The IP53 value 0 is a
  choice. :doc:`prom-image` gives the block layout.
- Bedrock revision 2 at 200 MHz: every published Origin 300, Origin 350,
  Fuel and Onyx 4 inventory reports ``HUB`` revision 2, *observed*. The
  raw register value behind that field is *unknown*.
- The IOC3 subsystem identity is ``0xc30a`` on the Fuel, the code Linux
  ``pci-xtalk-bridge.c`` assigns the IP34 board (*software expectation*),
  and ``0xffff`` on the Origin 300 (*unknown*).

Barcodes and serial numbers in board records are synthetic ``EMU``
values carrying a ``QEMU`` marker, not taken from a real machine.

.. _sgi-ip35-address-map:

IP35 physical address map (Origin 300, IP45)
============================================

This is the address map the Origin 300 and Fuel models
(``hw/mips/sgi/sn1.c`` and ``sn1-node.c``) follow: how an R10000-family
processor selects a space, how the node number sits in a physical
address, node memory, the crosstalk windows, the Bedrock's register
space, the hand-off and reset registers, and the interrupt-send address.
The IP53 profiles share it. The Bedrock units behind the register space
are on :doc:`hardware/bedrock`.

.. _sgi-ip35-address-map-spaces:

Processor-side spaces
---------------------

The processor selects a space with two bits beside the address, not
inside it: an R10000-family processor takes the uncached attribute from
virtual-address bits 58:57 of an uncached xkphys address and drives it
with the 40-bit physical address (OpenBSD ``cpu.h`` lines 92-113;
linux-ip35 ``spaces.h`` lines 13-16; Linux SN1 ``bedrock.h`` lines
40-44), *established*.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 18 10 26 46

   * - Space
     - Attribute
     - Base an unmapped R10000 guest uses
     - Source (all *established*)
   * - cached memory
     - none
     - ``0xa800_0000_0000_0000``
     - OpenBSD ``mnode.h`` line 40, ``cpu.h`` line 87, ``GENERIC-IP27``
       line 20; linux-ip35 ``spaces.h`` line 17; same value as the
       Origin 200.
   * - Hub special
     - 0
     - ``0x9000_0000_0000_0000``
     - ``cpu.h`` line 98, ``mnode.h`` line 46; ``spaces.h`` line 19.
   * - I/O
     - 1
     - ``0x9200_0000_0000_0000``
     - ``cpu.h`` line 99, ``mnode.h`` line 47, ``ip27_machdep.c`` line
       161; ``spaces.h`` line 20.
   * - memory special
     - 2
     - ``0x9400_0000_0000_0000``
     - ``cpu.h`` line 100, ``mnode.h`` line 48; ``spaces.h`` line 21.
   * - uncached memory
     - 3
     - ``0x9600_0000_0000_0000``
     - ``cpu.h`` line 101, ``mnode.h`` line 49; ``spaces.h`` line 22.

There are exactly four uncached spaces on the MIPS side. The Itanium
port's seven bases do not apply. The model carries the two attribute bits
above the 40-bit physical address in a 42-bit machine-bus address, as it
does for the Origin 200, an *approximation* of an internal path no
software sees.

   * - node window
     - 8 GB
     - 4 GB
     - the same lines; OpenBSD ``mnode.h`` line 55.
   * - maximum nodes
     - 128

     - ``sn1/arch.h`` ``MAX_NASIDS``; OpenBSD ``mnode.h`` line 438 (the
       64-node M-mode cap in ``sginode.c`` is an OpenBSD choice).
   * - Origin 200, for comparison
     - bits 39:32, 4 GB
     - bits 39:31, 2 GB
     - mainline Linux ``sn0/addrs.h`` lines 53-74.

The field diagnostics manual prints a sample configuration for an Origin
3000 pair in which node 1's cached memory base is
``0xa800000200000000``, 2^33 above node 0's
(:src:`108-0264-004 p. A-2`), agreeing with the seven-bit field at bit
33, *established*.

How a guest learns the mode differs across consumers. OpenBSD takes bit
18 of the network-interface port status register (offset ``0x680000``)
as "more nodes than M mode holds" (``sginode.c`` lines 100-103,
``hub.h`` line 218), the Origin 200 rule. The Bedrock register file
defines only bits 2:0 of that register (Linux SN1 ``sn1/hubni.h`` lines
1015-1033). The Itanium port reads the local block's system-size field,
bits 47:46 of its revision register, where 2 means N mode
(``hublb_next.h`` lines 19-32, ``ip37.c`` lines 34-38). OpenBSD prints
"Machine is in M mode" on real Origin 300s, one chassis and two cabled,
so bit 18 reading clear is *observed*. The model reads bit 18 clear and
resets the local-block field to 0. What an N-mode Bedrock returns, and
which register the PROM consults, are *unknown*.

.. _sgi-ip35-address-map-memory:

Node memory and the low-memory map
----------------------------------

Memory is reported per bank in 1 GB steps (OpenBSD ``sginode.c`` lines
549-590). The Linux SN1 headers give a 1 GB slot shift, a 64 MB minimum
and eight slots per node (``sn1/arch.h`` lines 47 and 53-54). OpenBSD
does not manage the first 64 MB of a node on IP35, against 32 MB on IP27
(``sginode.c`` lines 566-575, 519-528). All *established*.

The firmware's low-memory map, as the Linux SN1 tree draws it
(``kldir.h`` lines 164-283 for the diagram, 310-361 for the constants),
*established*:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 16 54 30

   * - Offset
     - Contents
     - Fixed-offset readers
   * - ``0x0000``
     - processor 0 exception handlers; cache-error frame at ``+0x400``,
       early cache-exception stack at ``+0x800``
     - firmware
   * - ``0x1000``
     - ARCS system parameter block, 1 KB
     - every ARCS guest
   * - ``0x2000``
     - low-memory directory, 1 KB, 16 entries of 64 bytes
     - OpenBSD ``mnode.h`` line 90; both Linux trees
   * - ``0x2400``
     - launch area: four entries of ``0x100``
     - firmware
   * - ``0x3000``
     - NMI handler page, four entries of ``0x40``
     - firmware
   * - ``0x8000``, ``0x10000``, ``0x18000``
     - processors 1 to 3: handlers, cache-error frame, early stack
     - firmware
   * - ``0x9000``, ``0xa000``

       processor

   * - ``0xb000``
     - global data area, 1 KB (directory slot 3)
     - guests via the directory
   * - ``0x20000``
     - processor-interface error spools, 64 KB (slot 6)
     - via the directory
   * - ``0x30000``
     - configuration graph (KLCONFIG), 64 KB (slot 1)
     - via the directory
   * - ``0x40000``

     - ``kldir.h`` line 336; linux-ip35 ``Platform`` line 11; OpenBSD

The directory slot numbers are unchanged from the Origin 200 (0 launch,
1 KLCONFIG, 2 NMI, 3 GDA, 4 free memory, 5 symmon stack, 6 PI error,

``mnode.h`` lines 91-100; both Linux trees). The stock IP35 PROM places
its configuration graph at ``0x30000``: the image reports
``init_klcfg: nasid 0 start 9600000000030000`` (:src:`ip35-prom-6.210`),
a *software expectation*. The Origin 200's offsets (NMI save area
``0x11400``, GDA ``0x11000``, KLCONFIG ``0x4000``, kernel ``0x19000``)
do not apply to IP35.

(``widget << 24``) plus the bridge's device window plus the register
offset. The device windows are two 2 MB windows from ``0x200000`` and
six 1 MB windows from ``0x600000`` (:doc:`hardware/xbridge`).

inherited text from the Origin 200 headers, and no IP35 consumer uses it
(linux-ip35 ``sn/addrs.h`` lines 119-133), *observed*. The cached-alias

(``ip27_machdep.c`` lines 338-345). Whether IP35 flips the bottom 128 KB

where a stock PROM places each processor's handler copies would settle
it.

four per-processor LED registers at ``0xc0`` and the board-identification
word at ``0x0400_0000``, and refuses the other offsets it does not decode
with a bus error. The evidence for each is on :doc:`hardware/bedrock`
and :doc:`hardware/l1`.

.. _sgi-ip35-address-map-interrupt:

Interrupt send
--------------

A widget raises a Bedrock interrupt by writing to an XIO address naming
the node, the Bedrock's own widget 1, the remote form and the
interrupt-pending-modify register: bit 47, the node shifted by 39 (38 on
IP27) and ``0x0180_0090`` (OpenBSD ``ip27_machdep.c`` lines 350-353;
Linux ``sn/xtalk/xtalkaddrs.h`` lines 66-91, adding ``0x200000`` for a
target on the second interface, ``hub_intr.c`` line 145). Writing
``0x100 | level`` sets, ``level`` alone clears (``hub.h`` lines 98-100;
Linux ``sn1/intr.h`` lines 122-143). The bridge's interrupt-destination
registers carry the Bedrock's crossbar widget number in bits 51:48
(``xbridge.c`` lines 2177-2191), from the configuration graph's crossbar
record, default ``0xa`` (``sginode.c`` lines 159-160, 338). All
*established*. For a Bridge-class bridge on a node other than 0, OpenBSD
places the node at bit 9 and linux-ip35 at bit 8 (``xbridge.c`` lines
1393-1394; ``pci-sgipic.c`` line 77); which is right is *unknown*.

.. _sgi-ip35-fuel:

The Fuel
========

The ``fuel`` machine builds the IP34 board: one Bedrock with one
processor, an XBridge whose head F carries the onboard ISP12160, IOC3 and
OHCI (the ``fuel`` preset of ``sgi-baseio``), an empty head E, an L1,
and the VPro graphics connector at crossbar widget 13
(:ref:`sgi-ip35-graphics`). The guide's rear panel shows no external XIO
connector (:src:`007-4480-001 p. 6`), *established*.

The IOC3 serves the guide's two serial ports, PS/2 keyboard and mouse,
parallel port and 10/100 Ethernet (:src:`007-4480-001 p. 35-38`),
*established*. An OpenBSD boot on a Fuel reports a DP83843 at MII
address 1 (:src:`miod-openbsd-sgi-story`), *observed*, and the model fits
that transceiver. The guide's lower connector, Serial 1, is IOC3 UART A,
*inferred* from OpenBSD's port map and a PROM menu captured on that
connector (:src:`graphica-o350-chimera-notes`).

The onboard Ethernet address comes from the L1. Without input it is the
``mac`` machine property's placeholder ``08:00:69:12:34:56``, in SGI's
OUI and no machine's factory value. It seeds the L1's address reply, the
component-1 board record and the IOC3. ``fuel-mac-eeprom=<file>``
replaces it with a 512-byte record image, and a ``-nic mac=`` that
disagrees with the record is refused. Where a physical Fuel stores this
record is *unknown*. :doc:`hardware/l1` has the record format.

A revision B Fuel board photograph shows a DS1742W, a 2 KiB clock part,
and OpenBSD's ``dsrtc.c`` comment names the same package, *observed*.
Its decode on the IOC3 byte bus is *unknown*, and OpenBSD calls its own
mapping a guess. The model fits the flat 32 KiB M48T35-compatible
timekeeper the Origin 300 uses, an *approximation*.

.. _sgi-ip35-numalink:

Origin 300 NUMAlink module and routed topologies
================================================

The Origin 300 topologies beyond one chassis:

``origin300-two-chassis``
  Two complete base modules, each with its own IP45 board, Bedrock,
  XBridge, IO-8 and L1, joined LINK connector to LINK connector by one
  NUMAlink 3 cable with no router, as the guide's "Connecting Two
  Servers" procedure builds them (:src:`007-4365-002 p. 38`),
  *established*. Two published cabled pairs show the per-module parts
  (:src:`marc-openbsd-sgi-139223952727384 lines 17-62`,
  :src:`nekonomicon-17314`), *observed*. The same cable carries the two
  L1s' RS-422 pair (:src:`108-0240-002 p. 2-47`), *established*. Accepted
  ``-smp`` splits are 2+2, 4+2, 2+4 and 4+4, and ``population=`` chooses
  between 4+2 and 2+4. The stock PROM numbers the nodes itself at every
  boot.

``origin300-pci-expansion``
  One base module cabled from its LINK connector to a PCI expansion
  module (:src:`007-4499-002 p. 12`), *established*. The expansion's
  Bedrock interface card (:doc:`hardware/bic`) has no processors or
  memory. Every launch binds a caller-owned 16 MiB raw image as the
  expansion's configuration storage with
  ``expansion-storage.0=expansion-enclosure=<drive>``. Its startup,
  power, register visibility, interrupt map and identity are
  *approximations* listed on :doc:`hardware/pci-expansion`.

``origin300-numalink-pci-expansion``
  Two base modules and a PCI expansion module through one logical
  eight-port SN1 router (:doc:`hardware/sn1-router`). The cables are
  selections: the first module's LINK to router port 1, the second's to
  port 6, the expansion's to port 4. The manual allows base modules on
  ports 1 and 6 and expansion modules only on ports 2 to 5
  (:src:`007-4498-002 p. 10-11, 96`), *established*. The router answers
  as chip revision 1, *inferred* from a six-chassis inventory's router
  "Revision 1" (:src:`nekonomicon-16727514`), and its L1 type ``0x52``
  is a choice. USB and L2 transport, router main power and reset
  propagation between modules are not modelled.

The NUMAlink module has eight external ports, A to H on the rear panel.
Ports 2 to 5 also carry USB and take base or PCI expansion modules;
ports 1, 6, 7 and 8 take base modules only. The module contains an L1
and a USB hub to the rack's L2. A documented system has two to eight
base modules in a tall rack (:src:`007-4498-002 p. 2, 10-16, 93-98,
130-133`). All *established*. The router chip's part number, ASIC count
and silicon revision are *unknown*. Linux SN1 headers name an
eight-port router family with chip identifier ``0x3017``, which ties to
this module only by *inference*. The ``origin300-numalink-module``
composition that records the full rack is a reference model;
:doc:`hardware/bricks` gives its tall-rack cabling.

=============

.. _sgi-ip35-origin350:

Origin 350
~~~~~~~~~~

The Origin 350 compute module holds two or four R16000 with 4 MB of
secondary cache each, eight DIMM slots in four pairs for 1 to 8 GB, one
Bedrock, one external NUMAlink 3 port, an L1 with a display, and the IO9
in the lowermost PCI slot (:src:`007-4566-001 p. 68-75, 114, 116`),
*established*. One published inventory reports the board
``030-1868-001`` revision C, a PIC carrier at widget 15 and IO9
``030-1771-005`` revision A (:src:`nekonomicon-16724207`), and another
reports Bedrock revision 2 (:src:`nekonomicon-16729141`), *observed*.

The profile's 8 MiB cache departs from the guide's 4 MB, and its 2 GiB
in two banks is not a captured population. Both are *approximations*.
The Bedrock's entry widget on an Origin 350 is *unknown*; the model uses
``0xa``. The profile names no netboot adapter.

.. _sgi-ip35-onyx350:

Onyx 350
~~~~~~~~

The profile builds one module, ``001c01``, of a published multi-module
Onyx 350 (:src:`graphica-onyx350-hinv`), without its graphics or the
other modules. The capture reports board ``IP53_4CPU`` ``030-2013-001``
revision D, four 800 MHz R16000 revision 3.0 with 8 MiB caches, Bedrock
revision 2 at 200 MHz, 8 GiB in eight logical 1 GiB banks, a
``2U_INT_53`` carrier ``030-1809-006`` revision D at widget 15 and IO9
``030-1771-006`` revision A, *observed*. The capture does not show the
module booting alone, and it reports PROM 6.211 where the model runs
6.210. The guide describes the base module's factory disk, four-port USB
card, IO9 and serial daughtercard (:src:`007-4632-001 p. 47, 50, 53`),
*established*. The capture does not tie them to this module, and none
of them is built. The netboot adapter is the BCM5701.

.. _sgi-ip35-tezro:

Tezro tower and rack
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The tower guide allows one, two or four processors with 4 MB of cache
and places IO9 at bus 1, slot 1. The two- and four-processor board has a
second XIO connector for an optional board; the one-processor board has
one (:src:`007-4564-001 p. 26-30, 38`). The rack guide gives the same
processor choices and 8 or 16 GiB in one or two enclosures
(:src:`007-4643-003 p. 21-32`). All *established*.

The profiles take their processors, memory and IO9 from a published
four-processor Tezro captioned as a deskside (:src:`graphica-tezro-hinv`):
``IP53_4CPU`` ``030-1868-001`` revision C, four 700 MHz R16000 revision
2.1 with 4 MiB caches, Bedrock revision 2, 4 GiB in six banks,
``WS_INT_53`` boards at widgets ``0xb`` and ``0xf``, and IO9
``030-1771-005`` revision A at ``0xf``, *observed*. Applying it to a
tower or a rack is an *inference* from the product family. An 800 MHz
Tezro inventory reports the same two interface-board widgets
(:src:`irixnet-post-26015`), *observed*.

``tezro-tower-multi`` builds two PICs, at widgets 11 and 15, with the
IO9 on the widget-15 PIC's head 0 and nothing on the other. The model
routes its widget-0 view through the PIC that owns the IO9, an
*approximation*: through the widget-11 PIC, the stock PROM could not
build its configuration for the node on the model. ``tezro-tower-one``
and ``tezro-rack`` build one PIC, at widget 15. The rack profile models
the first enclosure only. The netboot adapter is the BCM5701 on
``tezro-tower-multi`` and none on the other two.

.. _sgi-ip35-onyx4:

Onyx 4 host
~~~~~~~~~~~

An Onyx 4 host carries the system's PCI cards, including the boot IO9 at
bus 1, slot 1, and connects to G2N bricks over NUMAlink. A serial
terminal attaches to the host's L1 (:src:`007-4634-002 p. 1-2, 15,
20-23, 30-32`). All *established*. The profile takes module ``001c01`` of
a published inventory of a system labelled Onyx 350: board ``IP59_4CPU``
``030-1989-003`` revision C, four 1 GHz R16000 revision 3.0 with 16 MiB
caches, 8 GiB, and IO9 ``030-1771-006`` revision A at widget 15
(:src:`irixnet-thread-2263`), *observed*. The owner says a quad 1 GHz
board was installed in a G2N, and the inventory places that board at
``001c02``. Treating ``001c01`` as the Onyx 4 host is an *inference*.

The model maps this IP59 board onto the IP53 board path, its PIC and its
IO9 wiring, an *approximation*: no source describes the IP59 PIC, its
revision or its routes. It builds no G2, G2N or NUMAlink peer. The
``onyx4-host-g2`` and ``onyx4-host-g2n`` compositions are reference
models that no machine builds.

.. _sgi-ip53-pic:

PIC
~~~

Each PIC is one widget with two PCI-X heads, the second head's
registers ``0x800000`` above the first (OpenBSD ``xbridgereg.h`` lines
24-28 and ``xbowdevs``: part ``0xd102``, second head ``0xd112``,
manufacturer ``0x024``), *established*. The profiles select revision 3
behaviour and their direct-DMA settings as named compatibility profiles.
These do not establish the fitted PIC revision, its reset image or the
Bedrock-to-PIC wiring. :doc:`hardware/pic` describes the model and its
profiles.

IOC4
~~~~

IO9 carries one IOC4 function, ``10a9:100a``, at the existing model's
revision ``0x53``, with serial DMA enabled. The clock is 33,333,333 Hz on
the Origin 350, Onyx 350 and Onyx 4 host profiles and 66,666,667 Hz on
the Tezro profiles, all choices; the board straps are *unknown*. The L1
console and the four IOC4 serial ports are separate endpoints, as the
Onyx 350 and Onyx 4 guides describe. :doc:`hardware/ioc4` describes the
model.

.. _sgi-ip53-bcm570x:

BCM5701
~~~~~~~

IO9's Ethernet is the generic ``bcm570x`` model as a single-port BCM5701,
``14e4:1645``. The fitted IO9 Ethernet part and its revision are
*unknown*. :doc:`hardware/bcm570x` describes the model.

.. _sgi-ip53-ds1742:

.. _sgi-ip53-ds1744:

Timekeeper
~~~~~~~~~~

Every IP53 profile links a DS1744-compatible 32 KiB timekeeper
(:src:`analog-ds1744-rev-6-13`) to IOC4, mapped whole at IOC4 BAR0
offset ``0x80000`` with the clock in its last eight bytes, the span
OpenBSD's driver uses. A fresh or blank clock starts from the 1968 year
base. Its data and clock record are the ``nvram0`` and ``nvram0-clock``
stores. The fitted IO9 part and its address wiring are *unknown*. The
older DS1742 selection, which exposed only the clock bytes of a 2 KiB
part, is not chosen by any profile. :doc:`hardware/timekeepers`
describes both parts.

.. _sgi-ip53-io-profile:

Profile boundaries
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The Origin 350 and Onyx 350 captures are different boards in different
systems. Neither profile's topology or unmeasured behaviour is inferred
from the other, and the two-PIC Tezro layout is not inferred from an
Origin 350. A guest reaching the PROM menu or an IRIX login on one of
these profiles shows that the model runs that software, not that the
named product is wired that way.

.. _sgi-ip35-firmware:

Firmware and boot
=================

Every machine boots a stock IP35 PROM. ``-bios`` takes the raw payload or
SGI's distribution container, which the machine decodes
(:doc:`prom-image`). The payload must be exactly 1,476,264 bytes
(``0x1686a8``), the size of :src:`ip35-prom-6.210`.

Given only ``-bios``, the PROM's writes to its boot flash do not persist.
On the model the Origin 300 then prints the L1 banner and "Starting PROM
Boot process", resets, and repeats that sequence without reaching a
prompt. A writable boot flash fixes this. ``qemu-sgi-machine-init``
(:doc:`/tools/qemu-sgi-machine-init`) creates one, with the timekeeper
stores, from the same PROM image. With every store bound, ``-bios`` may
be left out.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Store
     - Size
     - Starts from
   * - ``node0-flash``
     - 16 MiB
     - the PROM image in the first 8 MiB flash position, with the
       product's configuration-block values, and the blank-media label
       in the second
   * - ``nvram0``
     - 32 KiB
     - zero; the IOC3 timekeeper on the Origin 300 and Fuel, the IO9
       timekeeper on the IP53 products
   * - ``nvram0-clock``
     - 16 bytes
     - zero

A two-node topology adds ``node1-flash``, and ``origin300-pci-expansion``
adds the expansion's own storage. :ref:`sgi-sn-storage` describes how
stores bind.

Where the processor's reset fetch reaches flash, which flash part is
fitted, and what answers outside the image are *unknown*.
:ref:`sgi-prom-image-ip35-views` gives the views the model maps and why.

The console is the L1's serial line, the first ``-serial``. The PROM
prints through the L1 and its maintenance menu appears there. On the
Origin 300 and Fuel the next serial lines are IOC3 ports A and B; on the
IP53 profiles they are the four IOC4 ports. The PROM keeps its console
choice in the boot flash, so it is firmware state, not a board property.

.. _sgi-ip35-running:

Running
=======

Each command below reached the stock 6.210 PROM's "System Maintenance
Menu" on the L1 line, on the model, within a 150-second timeout. Create
the stores once per machine, naming the selection as
``qemu-sgi-machine-init --machine help`` spells it:

.. code-block:: shell

   qemu-sgi-machine-init --machine origin300,topology=origin300,nodes=1 \
       --boot-prom ip35prom.img state

Then start the machine on them. For the Origin 300:

.. code-block:: shell

   qemu-system-mips64 -M origin300,node0-flash=f,nvram0=n,nvram0-clock=c \
       -blockdev driver=file,filename=state/node0-flash.raw,node-name=f \
       -blockdev driver=file,filename=state/nvram0.raw,node-name=n \
       -blockdev driver=file,filename=state/nvram0-clock.raw,node-name=c \
       -display none -nodefaults -monitor none -serial file:console.txt

The PROM reports two processors and 512 MB. The same pair of commands,
with each product's own state, runs the others; only the selection and
the start of ``-M`` change:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Product
     - ``qemu-sgi-machine-init --machine``
     - Start of ``-M``
     - PROM reports
   * - Fuel
     - ``fuel,topology=fuel,nodes=1``
     - ``fuel``, with or without ``-vga none``
     - one processor, 512 MB
   * - Origin 350
     - ``origin350,topology=origin350,nodes=1``
     - ``origin350``
     - four processors, 2048 MB
   * - Onyx 350
     - ``onyx350,topology=onyx350-compat,nodes=1``
     - ``onyx350,topology=onyx350-compat``
     - four processors, 8192 MB
   * - Tezro
     - ``tezro,topology=tezro-tower-multi,nodes=1``
     - ``tezro,topology=tezro-tower-multi``
     - four processors, 4096 MB
   * - Onyx 4 host
     - ``onyx4-host,topology=onyx4-host-serial-compat,nodes=1``
     - ``onyx4-host``
     - four processors, 8192 MB

Booting IRIX from here needs a disk on ``scsi.0``, the first onboard SCSI
controller, or a netboot through the IOC3 on the Origin 300 and Fuel or
the BCM5701 where the profile names it. :ref:`sgi-sn-launch-metadata`
lists each product's launch hints.

.. _sgi-ip35-graphics:

Graphics
========

``fuel`` fits a V12 VPro at its graphics connector by default, as
``-device sgi-odyssey,slot=widget-13,product=v12`` would; ``-vga none``
leaves the slot empty. A Fuel IRIX inventory and an OpenBSD capture both
place the VPro at widget 13 (:src:`tingo-fuel-irix-console`,
:src:`miod-openbsd-sgi-story`), *observed*. The captured Fuel carried a
V10, so fitting a V12 there is a choice.

``tezro-tower-multi`` and ``onyx350-compat`` take an optional V12 at
``widget-12``, ``-device sgi-odyssey,slot=widget-12,product=v12``, and
fit none by default. The published 700 MHz Tezro inventory reports its
V12, part ``030-1884-002`` revision D, at widget 12
(:src:`graphica-tezro-hinv`), *observed*. On the Onyx 350 the same
position is an alternative to its InfiniteReality, a choice. The pipe
links to that port of the crossbar of the PIC that owns widget 0, uses
the Bedrock's graphics-credit handling, and is marked alive once it
realizes. The route from either physical XIO connector to the Bedrock is
*unknown*. On the model, with a V12 fitted to ``tezro-tower-multi``, the
stock PROM finds the Odyssey board on nasid 0 and passes its
diagnostics.

The other IP35 topologies have no graphics slot. :ref:`sgi-sn-graphics-pipes`
describes how slots, defaults and refusals work, and
:doc:`hardware/odyssey` describes the board.

Open questions
==============

- The low bits of every product's board-identification word, and the
  board word, L1 type and L1 revision of any physical IP53 board.

- The PIC revision, reset state and Bedrock-to-PIC wiring on each IP53
  product, and IO9's PCI interrupt routes, endpoint revisions and clock
  straps.

  the TI PCI-to-PCI bridge and the other functions in the Tezro captures
  are.
- The R16000 reset mode word for each profile's clock and cache, and the
  Origin 350's fitted cache size.
- The Fuel's DS1742W decode, its interrupt wiring on revision G, where
  its Ethernet-address record lives, and its L1 type reply.
- The physical-to-software memory bank mapping on every product.
- How the reset fetch reaches flash, the fitted flash part, and which
  register the stock PROM reads on a warm reset.
- How a guest learns M or N mode, and whether a Bedrock still needs the
  widget-0 remap through big window 6.
- The NUMAlink module's router silicon and the L1's USB branch.
- The Origin 300 rear XIO connector's crossbar port.
