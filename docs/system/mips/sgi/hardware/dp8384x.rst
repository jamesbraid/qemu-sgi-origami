.. _sgi-dp8384x:

==============================================================
DP83840A and DP83843 Ethernet transceivers (Origin 200, 300)
==============================================================

The National Semiconductor DP83840A is the 10/100 Mb/s Ethernet
transceiver on the Origin 200 and Origin 2000 base I/O boards. The
DP83843 PHYTER is the transceiver on the Origin 300 IO-8 board and, in
the model, on Fuel. Both sit behind the IOC3's MII management registers
(:doc:`ioc3`) and are reached only through them. QEMU models both as one
type, ``dp83840``, in ``hw/net/dp83840.c`` and
``include/hw/net/dp83840.h``. The ``dp83843`` property selects the
DP83843. The IOC3 sets it from its ``phy-dp83843`` property and sets the
MII address from ``phy-addr``.

The DP83840A is modelled at the revision its identity register reports,
revision 1. The DP83843 is modelled as the DP83843BVJE, revision B
datasheet, identity revision 0. The model is a functional profile of the
management registers, not a revision-matched copy of either chip. Facts
are rated as described in :ref:`sgi-ratings`.

Published OpenBSD boot logs of Origin 200 machines print one transceiver
at MII address 31, ``nsphy0 at iec0 phy 31: DP83840 10/100 PHY, rev. 1``
(:src:`dmesgd-8994 nsphy line`), *observed*. OpenBSD prints that string
from the identity words, so the words ``0x2000`` and ``0x5c01`` follow:
OUI bits in word 2, and in word 3 the OUI remainder, model 0 and
revision 1, *inferred*. A photograph of an Origin 200 system board marks
the chip DP83840AVCE (:src:`theretroweb-origin200-image-1 Ethernet
section`), *observed*. That the Origin 2000 base I/O board carries the
same part at the same address is *inferred* from the shared IOC3 design.
No capture of an Origin 2000 transceiver was read for this page.

An Origin 300 capture prints ``nsphyter0 at iec0 phy 1: DP83843 10/100
PHY, rev. 0`` (:src:`marc-openbsd-sgi-139223952727384 line 173`),
*observed*. The DP83843 model number is 1 and the revision 0, which with
the shared OUI gives word 3 ``0x5c10`` :src:`ti-dp83843-snls021b p. 49`,
*established* for the documented product. That the board carries the
BVJE package, and not another DP83843 package, is *unknown*. The stock
Origin 300 PROM's power-on Ethernet diagnostic addresses only MII
address 1 and warns when word 3 is not ``0x5c10``, a *software
expectation*. Fuel uses the same selection. No inventory of a Fuel
transceiver was read, so its part and address are an *approximation*.

Software finds the transceiver by scanning all 32 addresses and taking
the first that answers, so exactly one address must answer. The model
answers only at its own address. At any other address the IOC3 returns
``0xffff`` and drops writes. The DP83840A address is fixed at the
``phy-addr`` value. The DP83843 address is the low five bits of
register ``0x19`` (see below) and starts at the configured value.

While the transceiver is in loopback, or (DP83843 only) isolated,
powered down or held in reset, the IOC3 exchanges no frames with the
network backend (:ref:`sgi-dp8384x-datapath`).

Writes to the read-only registers are dropped and log
``LOG_GUEST_ERROR``. Every other register reads zero and ignores writes,
and logs ``LOG_UNIMP``. A zero read, not ``0xffff``, keeps the part
looking present.

The reset values of registers ``0x00``, ``0x17`` and ``0x1c`` are the
DP83840A's documented defaults
(:src:`national-dp83840a-datasheet registers`), *established*. The
Origin 200 PROM's power-on transceiver test compares registers ``0x00``, ``0x17`` and ``0x1c``
against the same values and reads registers ``0x01``, ``0x05`` and
``0x06`` before it writes any control register, expecting no link, no
completed negotiation and no partner: BMSR ``0x7809`` under its mask,
and zero in the other two. These are *software expectations*, and the
model returns them. The register ``0x18`` reset value ``0x8020`` copies
the datasheet's default with its pin-latched bits modelled as normal
operation, an *approximation*: no source gives the strapping of the
board. The same test requires registers ``0x17``, ``0x18`` and ``0x1c``
to read back what was written, and the model stores them for that
reason. Their other functions are not modelled.

OpenBSD's ``nsphy`` driver read-modify-writes register ``0x17`` once on a
media change, setting the LED, congestion-control and transmit-ready
bits, restoring normal link-good operation and clearing the carrier
integrity monitor. Nothing reads it back for a decision, a *software
expectation*. Bit 5 of register ``0x17`` on this part is the carrier
integrity monitor disable, so writing it must not select loopback.

Reset values and masks are *established* for the documented DP83843BVJE
and follow the page numbers above. Where the datasheet's strap inputs
choose a reset value, the model picks one profile: copper, normal
encoding, nibble-mode 10 Mb/s, node (not repeater), all four modes
advertised. The actual board straps are *unknown*, so the reset values
that depend on them are an *approximation*. The datasheet's own strap
prose for the BMCR reset value contradicts its table. The model keeps
``0x3100``.

The Fuel PROM's power-on Ethernet diagnostic expects zero in register
``0x17`` and zero in register ``0x18`` under mask ``0xc77f``, a *software
expectation*. The profile's reset values satisfy it. The model's BMSR
``0x7849`` and ANER ``0x0004`` follow the datasheet's permanent bits.

ANNPTR resets to ``0x2001``, not the nominal table's ``0x2801``. The
datasheet's published erratum for the BVJE initialises the transmit
toggle to zero :src:`ti-dp83843-snls021b p. 43`, *established*. The
model does not exchange next pages. The toggle never changes.

Registers ``0x08``-``0x0f``, ``0x1a``, ``0x1b`` and ``0x1d``-``0x1f``
read zero and ignore writes. Register ``0x1c`` (the DP83840A's
10BASE-T configuration) is reserved on this part. All of them log
``LOG_UNIMP``. The datasheet gives no reset readback for them, so the
zero is an *approximation*.

PHY status
----------

Register ``0x10`` reports the state the model has, from the same
predicates BMSR uses:

- bit 0, link: set when the live link is up;
- bit 1, 10 Mb/s, and bit 2, full duplex: valid only with link up, and
  either autonegotiation disabled or negotiation complete. They come
  from BMCR when forced and from the resolved mode when negotiated;
- bit 3, loopback: set when the model is in loopback;
- bit 5, autonegotiation complete: set with link up and autonegotiation
  enabled;
- bit 8, interrupt pending: reads the shared pending flag and clears
  it;
- bit 9, autonegotiation enabled: follows BMCR;
- bit 11, device ready: set with link up and not isolated, an
  *approximation*. The datasheet's timing for ready is not modelled.

The receive-error, carrier-integrity, false-carrier, remote-fault,
jabber and page-received bits read zero. The model generates none of
those events.

Counters and control fields
---------------------------

The three counters store any value written and clear on read. The model
never increments them.

Register ``0x16`` bit 14 (far-end fault indication) reads zero while
autonegotiation is enabled. With autonegotiation disabled and fibre
selected in register ``0x19`` bit 5 it reads one. Writes cannot change
it. Register ``0x17`` bit 12 reads one when fibre is selected. The
datasheet's carrier-integrity status bit (register ``0x16`` bit 4)
reads zero because no event sets it.

Register ``0x18`` bit 7 reads one when the link is full duplex or
register ``0x19`` bit 9 selects repeater mode, whatever was written to
it.

Transmit-quiet, test-pattern, LED, equalizer, baseline-wander and other
analogue controls keep their written bits but do nothing. The model
logs ``LOG_UNIMP`` for a write to register ``0x16`` with any bit set but
bit 5, to ``0x17`` with bit 14, to ``0x18`` with any of ``0x055c``, to
``0x19`` with any bit above the address, and to BMCR with the collision
test bit (bit 7).

.. _sgi-dp8384x-reset:

Reset
=====

BMCR bit 15 resets the transceiver at once and the bit never reads set.
The datasheet gives a reset time of about 1.2 ms in one place and about
500 microseconds in another :src:`ti-dp83843-snls021b p. 37`. The model
does not delay either.

Reset restores every register to its reset value, drops pending
negotiation and the pending interrupt flag, and cancels the timers. The
DP83843 reset also restores the configured MII address even if software
had moved it. Device reset does the same. A management reset sent to
the old address after software moved the DP83843 does not reach it.

The Origin 300 IO-8 composition holds the DP83843 in reset from the
IOC3's GPIO5 line. With GPIO5 configured as an output and driven low the
IOC3 asserts a logical reset request. Driving it high, or disabling the
output, releases it. Release does not restart anything. The public
OpenBSD IOC3 driver pulses GPIO5 low and high before it attaches the
Ethernet driver, a *software expectation*. The board's wiring and polarity are
*unknown*. The connection is an *approximation*. Fuel has no such
connection, and the DP83840A ignores the line.

While held, the DP83843:

- reads as absent (``0xffff``) and ignores writes, so no register side
  effect runs;
- reports no link, completion or readiness;
- passes no frames.

A MAC reset leaves the transceiver alone.

.. _sgi-dp8384x-link:

Link and negotiation
====================

There is no cable in the model. Link, partner ability and timing are
synthesised, an *approximation* throughout. The partner is a
cooperative far end that advertises ``0x01e1`` (all four modes plus the
selector) and does not fail. Link state follows the network backend: a
backend link-down clears the live link, and link-up restores the
retained result without renegotiating.

DP83840A
--------

Out of reset the link reads down with negotiation incomplete. The
Origin 200 PROM's power-on test requires that state, a *software
expectation*. Two things bring the link up:

- a BMSR read with autonegotiation enabled starts a 2 second
  virtual-time timer, once per reset. When it fires negotiation reads as
  complete and link as up. Two seconds lies in the datasheet's range for
  negotiation and outlasts the PROM test's reset-value sweep, which
  takes about a tenth of a second, an *approximation*. Starting it from
  reset instead would expire before the PROM test's probe, and starting
  it from a control write never fires under Linux's phylib;
- any BMCR write that does not isolate the transceiver brings link up
  and marks negotiation complete at once and cancels the timer. A write
  that sets isolate marks negotiation incomplete and cancels the timer.
  A later BMSR read starts it again.

Both drivers read BMSR twice and combine the reads, because hardware
latches link low. The model reports the live level in both reads, an
*approximation*, so nothing latches and nothing needs clearing.

With the link up, OpenBSD's ``nsphy`` checks the completion bit when
autonegotiation is enabled, then reads ANER. If the partner can
autonegotiate it intersects ANAR and ANLPAR to get 100BASE-TX full
duplex. Only otherwise does it read register ``0x19`` to tell 10 from
100 Mb/s, and its own comment says that register does not report
duplex. The model reports a capable partner, so a guest takes the first
route. A forced-speed write also reports completion in BMSR, which a
real chip would not.

DP83843
-------

The first-BMSR-read timer works as on the DP83840A. Negotiation resolves
ANAR against the partner in priority order 100BASE-TX full duplex,
100BASE-TX half duplex, 10BASE-T full duplex, 10BASE-T half duplex
:src:`ti-dp83843-snls021b p. 29`, *established*. The result is held until
reset, a restart, or autonegotiation being disabled. A later ANAR write
changes the next negotiation, not the held result, an *approximation*:
the datasheet does not fix the update point. With no common mode, link
and completion stay down and the model logs ``LOG_UNIMP``.

A BMCR write that enables autonegotiation without the restart bit, and
does not isolate, completes negotiation at once, as on the DP83840A.

A BMCR write that sets autonegotiation enable with restart (bit 9) and
does not isolate begins a timed restart, an *approximation*:

1. negotiation reads as incomplete and link as down;
2. for 1.5 seconds of virtual time, the break-link interval, BMCR bit 9
   reads one;
3. bit 9 then clears and the model negotiates for 2 seconds more;
4. negotiation completes 3.5 seconds after the write.

The datasheet gives about 1.5 seconds of break-link and about 2 to 3
seconds of negotiation :src:`ti-dp83843-snls021b p. 30-31`,
*established*. It does not say whether the negotiation time includes
break-link, when bit 9 clears, or how loopback changes the start. The
model's values, and clearing bit 9 at the end of break-link, are its
choice. Reading BMSR neither starts nor advances the timers. Clearing
bit 9 in software leaves the restart running. Clearing autonegotiation
enable cancels it and forces the link up at once to the BMCR speed and
duplex, provided the backend link is up and the media configuration is
supported. A forced link reports link up and completion down.
Disabling autonegotiation is therefore not a cancel of a physical
break-link wait, an *approximation*.

The "unsupported media configuration" test used throughout is:
repeater or fibre selected in register ``0x19`` (mask ``0x0220``),
serial 10 Mb/s, autoswitch or AUI selected in register ``0x18`` (mask
``0x1a20``), or a bypass or non-normal 100 Mb/s data-path selection in
register ``0x17`` (mask ``0x3f80``). Any of them leaves the link down
with the written value kept, logs ``LOG_UNIMP`` and invalidates the held
negotiation result. Returning to a supported setting does not restore
it by itself.

Power-down (BMCR bit 11) drops link. Isolate is covered below.

.. _sgi-dp8384x-datapath:

Data path and loopback
======================

BMCR bit 14 loops transmit back to receive on both parts. The IOC3
delivers the looped frame through its own receive path and sends nothing
to the backend, whatever the link state. Frames arriving from the
backend are refused while the loop is selected.

Other loopback selections are part-specific:

- DP83840A: register ``0x18`` bits ``0x0300`` or ``0x0800``. The
  DP83840A's register ``0x17`` is storage, so a write to it never
  selects loopback. Origin 200 software depends on that, because OpenBSD
  writes the carrier-integrity bit there on every media change;
- DP83843: register ``0x17`` bits 5 (twister loopback) and 4 (10 Mb/s
  ENDEC loopback). Register ``0x18`` is storage here. The Origin 300
  PROM's twister loopback test writes BMCR ``0x2100`` and then ORs 0x20
  into register ``0x17``, a *software expectation*. OpenBSD never
  writes the register. Setting BMCR bit 14 sets both LBR bits and
  clears the 100 Mb/s data-path selection (bits 9:7)
  :src:`ti-dp83843-snls021b p. 33`, *established*. The datasheet does
  not say what bits 5 and 4 read after BMCR loopback is cleared again. The model leaves them set,
  so loopback stays selected until software clears them or the
  transceiver resets, an *approximation*.

Isolate (BMCR bit 10) and power-down (bit 11) stop frames in both
directions on the DP83843 while management access keeps working
*(established)*. An unsupported media configuration and reset hold do
the same in the model. On the DP83840A the data path is always on.
Clearing the condition re-opens receive and the IOC3 flushes frames it
queued meanwhile.

Interrupts
==========

Neither part drives an interrupt line in the model. The DP83840A has an
interrupt facility the board does not use. On the DP83843 the pending
flag exists only in registers ``0x10`` bit 8 and ``0x12`` bit 15, which
share one flag. Reading either returns the old value and clears it.
Writing either does nothing.

With register ``0x11`` bit 1 (enable) set, the flag is raised by a
change of live link in either direction and by negotiation completion
going from clear to set. Clearing completion on a restart or a forced
write is not an event. Reset clears the flag and the enable. Clearing
the enable later leaves a raised flag in place, an *approximation*: the
datasheet gives no rule.

Register ``0x11`` bit 0 (test interrupt) raises the flag at the end of
every access to the transceiver, independent of the enable. A read first
returns its value and clears the flag, and then bit 0 raises it again.
Accesses that miss the address do not count. The order is a model
choice for deterministic tests, not a measured timing.

Errors and logging
==================

- Writes to read-only registers (BMSR, the identity registers, ANLPAR,
  ANER, and on the DP83840A register ``0x19``): ``LOG_GUEST_ERROR``. The
  DP83843 drops writes to registers ``0x10`` and ``0x12`` silently.
- Registers and fields the model does not implement: ``LOG_UNIMP``, as
  listed above.
- Reads of unimplemented DP83840A registers return zero, not ``0xffff``.
- A DP83843 with no common advertised mode, or with an unsupported media
  configuration, logs ``LOG_UNIMP`` and leaves the link down.
- Management accesses at an unmatched address return ``0xffff`` or are
  dropped without logging.

Migration
=========

Identical builds only. The DP83840A state (control, advertisement, next
page, the three storage registers, negotiation and link flags, restart
phase and deadline, and the settle timer) migrates. A subsection
carries the DP83843 registers, counters, resolved mode, pending flag and
reset hold. The configured address and the part selection are
properties, not state. Saving during a pending restart or settle timer
and restoring on the same build resumes it.

Model limits
============

- No analogue behaviour: signal quality, polarity, squelch, jabber,
  carrier integrity, remote fault generation and the test patterns.
  Selecting them logs ``LOG_UNIMP``.
- No real link partner. Negotiation always succeeds against an
  all-capable partner when ANAR shares a mode. Next-page exchange,
  parallel detection and partner faults do not exist.
- Link status is the live level and never latches low.
- The DP83840A's registers ``0x17``, ``0x18`` and ``0x1c`` are storage.
  Its register ``0x19`` does not report speed.
- The strap-dependent reset values of both parts are the model's profile,
  not measurements of any board.
- Reset time, break-link and negotiation times are the model's choices
  within the datasheet's ranges.
- The Fuel board's transceiver is a catalogue selection, and it has no
  GPIO reset connection.

Tests
=====

``tests/qtest/sn0-ioc3-test.c`` covers the DP83840A on the Origin 200
IOC3: the single answering address
(``sn0/ioc3/phy-answers-at-one-address``), the identity words, the
self-clearing reset, the bench state before a control write, a usable
link after one, the 2 second settle on a status read, the configuration
register read-back and address field, the diagnostic's reset values and
the loopback register.

``tests/qtest/sgi-ioc3-eth-test.c`` covers both parts through the IOC3's
Ethernet engine. DP83843 tests cover the twister loopback turning a
frame round, loopback requiring bit 5, a reset re-opening the backend,
the next-page transmit register, BMCR loopback selecting LBR, the
restart timing, cancel and repeat cases, the forced-mode link, the
register subset (reset values, masks, modes, events and address) and
migration at each restart phase. Two tests assert that the DP83840A is
unchanged: bit 5 of register ``0x17`` does not select loopback, and a
restart behaves as before.

``tests/qtest/sn1-address-map-test.c`` has ``origin300/ioc3/phy-gpio-reset``
for the IO-8 GPIO5 reset, including the held and released states, the
unrelated-pin and disabled-output cases, and that a MAC reset does not
reset the transceiver. ``tests/qtest/sn1-fuel-test.c`` has
``fuel/ioc3-phy``, which checks the DP83843 identity and reset values at
address 1 on Fuel and that the IO-8 GPIO5 reset is not connected.
